# Use the scan; the ABC turned out to be genuine too

`imslp-pmlus08051-berlin-1929.pdf` is the **1929 Irving Berlin Inc. first edition**,
voice and piano, from IMSLP (listed there as public domain), obtained by clicking
through their copyright disclaimer by hand. It is the authority and the song file
was written from it.

The chorus begins on **printed page 3** (PDF page index 2), third system, marked
`Chorus`, **four flats** and **cut time**. Four flats is **F minor** here, not
A-flat major -- the opening triad, the bass alternation and the final chord all
say so. The verse precedes it and is not wanted (SPEC §2). The ukulele frames have
no letter names, but the title page prints the tuning (A D F# B, capo 1), so they
are decodable; the harmony in the song file was nonetheless read off the piano
part, because the sheet prints no chord letters at all.

**A correction worth keeping.** The other cached file --
`abcnotation-...-contra-tune-puttinontheritz-am-0000.abc` -- was distrusted before
the scan arrived, on the grounds that it sits in a contra-dance directory, has no
composer line, no corroborating second transcription, and a melody written with
octave drops that read as fiddle figuration. **It is the right tune.** Its opening
bars transpose exactly onto the scan's bar 1 once its note values are doubled: its
2/4 barring puts two of its bars to one of the song's. The octave drop is Berlin's.
Being in a dance directory was not evidence of anything.
